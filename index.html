Automated Zebrafish Behavioral Classification via Multi-Architecture Deep Feature Fusion
Sofiat Adeyemi, Omer Soysal — Dept. of Computer Science, Southeastern Louisiana University · March 2026

A systematic comparison of three deep learning architectures — 2D CNN (spatial), 3D CNN (spatiotemporal), and a convolutional autoencoder (unsupervised) — for classifying zebrafish behavioral responses to pharmacological stimuli (anesthetic, stimulant, control) from fluorescence microscopy stacks. Key finding: temporal motion patterns, not static spatial features, encode pharmacological signal. Best model: 3D CNN + Logistic Regression, 93.19% CV accuracy, 68.18% test accuracy, AUC 0.927, 16% improvement over frame-level CNN. Saliency analysis confirmed biologically relevant attention (anterior region, yolk sac). Statistical validation: ANOVA p < 0.05 (Bonferroni-corrected), Cohen's d > 1.0.

RLVR for Math Reasoning in GPT-2-Class Models
Sofiat Adeyemi, Kazim Sekeroglu — MindSea Lab, Southeastern Louisiana University

Does reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards — a binary correct/incorrect signal on GSM8K — actually improve math reasoning in small models, and which method works best? Starting from SFT at four GPT-2 scales (124M, 355M, 774M, 1.5B), we apply PPO, GRPO, DPO, and RFT, then rerun RL from the stronger RFT checkpoint to test whether a better starting point changes the results. A Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B anchor separates old architecture from small model as an explanation for GPT-2's limits. Evaluated on GSM8K, SVAMP, and ASDiv with 3 seeds, significance testing, an error breakdown, and a compute-efficiency table. Prior work either uses much larger models or tests only one method; this study compares methods head to head and reports honestly whether RL helps at this scale.

Cross-Tokenizer Distillation for Small-Scale Math Reasoning
Sofiat Adeyemi, Kazim Sekeroglu — MindSea Lab, Southeastern Louisiana University

Qwen2.5-Math-7B has a ~152K-token vocabulary and GPT-2 has 50K, with almost no overlap, so teacher logits cannot simply be copied to the student. We compare four ways of handling this on 124M, 355M, and 774M students: GRPO alone; GRPO + text-level chain-of-thought (decode the teacher's reasoning and train with cross-entropy, sidestepping the mismatch); GRPO + logit distillation through a learned linear projection from the teacher vocabulary to the student vocabulary; and logit distillation from the text-level checkpoint. The vocabulary projection is the primary technical contribution — a general method for distilling across tokenizer families. This paper is the companion to the RL study: that one establishes what RL alone can do at this scale, and this one asks how much a math-specialized teacher adds on top.

Making Small Language Models Reason Effectively Through Post-Training Methods
Sofiat Adeyemi · Thesis Advisor: Dr. Kazim Sekeroglu · MindSea Lab, Southeastern Louisiana University

Large language models exhibit emergent mathematical reasoning, but whether post-training can induce this in small models remains poorly understood. The thesis is organized as two companion papers on GPT-2-class models, with all training pipelines implemented from scratch without high-level training wrappers.

Paper 1 — RLVR for Math Reasoning in GPT-2-Class Models. Does RL from a binary correct/incorrect reward on GSM8K improve math reasoning in small models, and which method works best? After SFT at four GPT-2 scales (124M–1.5B), PPO, GRPO, DPO, and RFT are applied and compared, with a second experiment starting RL from the stronger RFT checkpoint. A Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B anchor separates the effect of an older architecture from the effect of small size. Evaluation covers GSM8K, SVAMP, and ASDiv with 3 seeds, significance testing, an error breakdown, and a compute-efficiency table.

Paper 2 — Cross-Tokenizer Distillation for Small-Scale Math Reasoning. Qwen2.5-Math-7B (~152K tokens) and GPT-2 (50K) share almost no vocabulary, so logits cannot be copied directly. Four conditions are compared on 124M, 355M, and 774M students: GRPO alone, GRPO with text-level chain-of-thought, GRPO with logit distillation through a learned linear vocabulary projection, and logit distillation from the text-level checkpoint. The projection is the primary technical contribution, a general method for distilling across tokenizer families.

The two papers are intentionally linked: Paper 1 establishes what RL alone can do at this scale, and Paper 2 asks how much a math-specialized teacher can add on top.

Automated Zebrafish Behavioral Classification via Multi-Architecture Deep Feature Fusion
Sofiat Adeyemi · Advisor: Dr. Omer Soysal · Dept. of Computer Science, Southeastern Louisiana University

Zebrafish are a key model organism in pharmacological neuroscience, but manual behavioral annotation is time-intensive and subjective. This work addresses automated classification across anesthetic, stimulant, and control conditions — a computer vision problem where temporal motion patterns, not static spatial features, encode the pharmacological signal.

Three architectures compared — 2D CNN, 3D CNN (16-frame), convolutional autoencoder — on ~1,200 frames from 30 TIFF stacks. Best model: 3D CNN + Logistic Regression, 93.19% CV accuracy, 68.18% test accuracy, AUC 0.927, +16% over frame-level CNN. Saliency maps confirmed anatomically appropriate attention.
